About the group

CHIRP (Chip-scale [Placeholder Expansion of Acronym]) is a photonics research group at Bridgewater State University focused on the design and fabrication of nonlinear integrated photonic circuits. Our work bridges behavioral models with physical chip layouts — moving from custom simulation frameworks, through rapid 2D prototyping, to high-fidelity 3D electromagnetic simulation and foundry-ready tape-outs.

We collaborate across institutions — including MIT and AIM Photonics for fabrication, and partner universities in Colombia — and welcome students and researchers interested in combining experimental, theoretical, and computational approaches to integrated optics.

Focus areas

What we work on

Four connected threads that take a photonic circuit from concept to fabricated chip.

Inverse Design

Gradient-based inverse-design methodologies for optimizing nonlinear photonic structures beyond what intuition-driven design can reach.

[Visual placeholder]

Nonlinear Photonic Circuits

Devices exploiting nonlinear material platforms (e.g. SiN, GeSbS) for next-generation on-chip photonic functionality.

[Visual placeholder]

Foundry Tape-outs

Fabrication-ready layouts submitted to foundry partners, including collaborations with AIM Photonics on 220 nm SiN platforms.

[Visual placeholder]

Simulation Frameworks

Custom Python tooling and full-wave EM simulation (Tidy3D, Lumerical, Meep) spanning 2D rapid prototyping to high-fidelity 3D models.
